Why journals fail

A blank page on a Tuesday night is a terrible way to remember your life.

Most journalling apps hand you an empty box and hope you feel inspired. You don’t, so you stop, and the year vanishes anyway. Memoires starts at the other end. Your camera roll already holds a better record of last February than your memory does.

How it works

It starts the entry. You finish it.

  1. 1

    It reads the camera roll

    In the background, on your iPhone, it groups recent photos by time and place: a weekend away, or a Saturday that clearly wasn’t ordinary. None of it leaves the phone.

  2. 2

    It nudges you at the right moment

    A quiet notification: you haven’t written about Lisbon yet. There’s a daily reminder too, if you want one. Both are scheduled on the phone itself, with no push server involved.

  3. 3

    The entry is half-written already

    You open it and the photos, the date and the place are already there. Type, or dictate it out loud while you’re walking home. Two minutes later you have the entry.

Features

The rest of the app

Trip & event detection

Most journals wait for you to open them. Memoires groups photos that cluster in time and place, then offers you the ones that look like a trip or an occasion. It runs the comparison on your iPhone, in the background, and never uploads a photo to do it.

Photo-led entries

Pull in shots straight from your camera roll, or take one in the app. A photo and two lines is a finished entry.

Voice dictation

Talk instead of typing. For most languages the transcription runs on your iPhone, so the recording never reaches a server.

Refine with AI

Tidy up a rambling entry into something readable. It’s a paid extra, and it only ever touches the single entry you tap it on.

Timeline & calendar

Scroll it as one long stream, or step back to a month at a time.

Mood & place

Tag how the day felt and where it happened. The location sits inside the entry on your phone and never reaches a server.

Find any memory

Search everything you’ve written for a name or a city, and open the day it happened.

Locked, and yours

Face ID on the app if you want it, and optional iCloud sync across your own devices.

Privacy

A journal you can be honest in.

Reading someone’s photo library is a lot to ask. So the scanning happens on your iPhone and the results stay there. Memoires makes its money from subscriptions.

Read the full privacy policy
Stored on your device
Entries, photos, moods and places live locally in the app. iCloud sync is off until you turn it on.
Photos are never uploaded
Not for the scanning, not for the suggestions. They do not leave your iPhone.
One clearly-marked exception
Refine with AI sends the text of that one entry to be rewritten, and only when you tap it. Nothing else goes with it.
No ads, no profiling
No advertising, and nothing sold to data brokers. The anonymous usage stats can be switched off in Settings.
